# Full legal permission box for calls, texts and marketing

One tick box on the survey, written to meet the rules carriers and the phone regulators expect before a business can call, text or email someone. Nothing about the survey flow changes, only the wording and what we keep on file as proof.

## The new wording

> **Required.** I authorize **Savvy Swim**, a Santana and Rivera Company, and its service technicians to contact me at the phone number and email address I provided, including by live call, automatic telephone dialing system, artificial or prerecorded voice, text message (SMS and MMS) and email, about this request, my free inspection, appointment and service updates, and about marketing such as promotions, seasonal pool reminders, service offers and company news.
>
> I understand consent is not a condition of purchasing any goods or services, and I may still request service by calling 817-663-7665. Message frequency varies, typically fewer than 10 messages per month. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to stop texts at any time, or HELP for help. Every email carries an unsubscribe link. Carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages. Calls may be recorded or monitored for quality and training. I am at least 18 years old and I am the subscriber or customary user of the number I provided.
>
> I have read and agree to the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy), the [Terms and Conditions](/terms-and-conditions) and the [Text Message Terms](/terms-and-conditions#sms).

The box is never ticked in advance and the survey cannot be sent without it, which is what makes the consent valid.

## Proof on file

For every survey lead we keep the exact wording the person agreed to, the moment they agreed, the page they were on, their device and their internet address. That record is what settles a dispute if anyone ever claims they did not opt in.

## Text message terms section

The Terms page gets a short, plainly written texting section: who sends the messages, what they are about, how often, that rates may apply, STOP and HELP, how to get help, and the carrier disclaimer. The consent box links straight to it.

## Where it applies

The survey page uses the new single box. The other forms keep their current two-part wording, since they already separate the reply permission from the marketing permission. Say the word if you want them switched over too.
